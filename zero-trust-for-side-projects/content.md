Defense in depth means assuming every layer will fail. When one does, the next one catches it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Core Problem

Security and convenience are not opposites. Good security should be invisible to legitimate users.

Security is not a feature you add at the end. It is a property that emerges from every decision you make throughout the system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Learned

The most common security vulnerabilities are not sophisticated attacks. They are simple mistakes: exposed secrets, missing validation, excessive permissions.

The pattern repeats across every project I have worked on. The mechanical
work gets easier and faster, but the decisions that frame it remain
stubbornly human. Tools change. Judgment endures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Closing Thoughts

There is no shortcut to good engineering. There are better tools, faster
feedback loops, and more powerful abstractions. But the work of understanding
the problem, choosing the right tradeoffs, and building something that lasts
remains fundamentally a human endeavor.

The best advice I can offer is this: invest in your judgment. The rest
can be automated.
