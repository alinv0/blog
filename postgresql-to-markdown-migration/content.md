The best architecture is the simplest one that solves your actual problems. Not your imagined future problems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Core Problem

Distributed systems add complexity at every layer. Make sure the complexity pays for itself.

Good architecture makes the right thing easy and the wrong thing hard. Bad architecture makes everything equally difficult.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Learned

Architecture is the set of decisions that are expensive to change. Every other decision can be deferred.

The pattern repeats across every project I have worked on. The mechanical
work gets easier and faster, but the decisions that frame it remain
stubbornly human. Tools change. Judgment endures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Closing Thoughts

There is no shortcut to good engineering. There are better tools, faster
feedback loops, and more powerful abstractions. But the work of understanding
the problem, choosing the right tradeoffs, and building something that lasts
remains fundamentally a human endeavor.

The best advice I can offer is this: invest in your judgment. The rest
can be automated.
