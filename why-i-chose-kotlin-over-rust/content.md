Code is a liability, not an asset. Every line you write needs to be maintained, tested, and eventually replaced. Write less, think more.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Core Problem

The gap between junior and senior engineers is not about knowing more syntax or frameworks. It is about recognizing patterns, anticipating problems, and making tradeoffs intentionally.

Software engineering is fundamentally about making decisions under uncertainty. Every line of code represents a choice, and the quality of those choices determines the quality of the system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Learned

The best engineers I have worked with share one trait: they know when not to build something. Restraint is as important as capability.

The pattern repeats across every project I have worked on. The mechanical
work gets easier and faster, but the decisions that frame it remain
stubbornly human. Tools change. Judgment endures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Closing Thoughts

There is no shortcut to good engineering. There are better tools, faster
feedback loops, and more powerful abstractions. But the work of understanding
the problem, choosing the right tradeoffs, and building something that lasts
remains fundamentally a human endeavor.

The best advice I can offer is this: invest in your judgment. The rest
can be automated.
