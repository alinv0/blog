AI-assisted development is not about replacing developers. It is about amplifying the decisions that developers already make.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The Core Problem

The quality of AI output depends entirely on the quality of the constraints you provide. Garbage context in, garbage code out.

Large language models are remarkably good at mechanical translation between known patterns. They are remarkably bad at knowing when a pattern does not apply.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Learned

The most productive AI workflow I have found is not asking AI to write code from scratch. It is asking AI to implement a design I have already thought through.

The pattern repeats across every project I have worked on. The mechanical
work gets easier and faster, but the decisions that frame it remain
stubbornly human. Tools change. Judgment endures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Closing Thoughts

There is no shortcut to good engineering. There are better tools, faster
feedback loops, and more powerful abstractions. But the work of understanding
the problem, choosing the right tradeoffs, and building something that lasts
remains fundamentally a human endeavor.

The best advice I can offer is this: invest in your judgment. The rest
can be automated.
